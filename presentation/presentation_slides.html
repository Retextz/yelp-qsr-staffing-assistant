  • Industry: Quick-Service Restaurant (QSR) & Food Service
  • Intended User: Regional Operations Manager managing 50+ locations
  • Main Problem: Hourly foot traffic varies dramatically by shift, but staffing schedules are set uniformly. Understaffing during peak shifts causes long wait times and negative reviews; overstaffing wastes labor cost.

Core Analytical Skills

3. Required Skills: Explore, Compare, & Model

  • Skill 1 (Explore Data): Univariate EDA showing strong weekly seasonality (Friday-Sunday peaks) and a 3.7 mean star rating benchmark.
  • Skill 2 (Compare Two Groups): Welch's t-test showing Quick Service averages significantly higher hourly check-ins than Full Service (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.52).
  • Skill 3 (Statistical Model): Negative Binomial Count Regression fitting overdispersed traffic, explaining shift variations with Pseudo R² = 0.31.
Speaker Notes (1:00 min): "Our assistant relies on three required statistical skills: Skill 1 explores overall baseline distributions; Skill 2 compares Quick Service against Full Service using Welch's t-test, proving QSRs handle higher foot traffic without lower ratings; and Skill 3 fits a Negative Binomial regression model to handle severe count overdispersion, predicting hourly traffic based on shift hour, day, and review volume."

Project Limitations

8. What We Cannot Conclude

  • Data Limitation: Yelp check-ins are self-reported smartphone activity; they proxy foot traffic but undercount total customer walk-ins.
  • Statistical Limitation: Regression and spatial correlations show observational associations, not direct causation.
  • Implementation Limitation: Quarto reports reflect static snapshots and require re-rendering when new data arrives.
Speaker Notes (0:30 min): "Finally, we maintain honest statistical limitations: Yelp check-ins are a digital proxy for foot traffic and may undercount non-smartphone users. Our regression models establish strong predictive association but not causation. Thank you, and I am ready to take any questions!"
